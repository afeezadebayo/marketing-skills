# Creator Partnerships and Employee Advocacy

People trust people more than brands. Content from creators, employees, founders and customers usually reaches further and persuades more than posts from brand accounts, because audiences follow people they relate to. Creator partnerships put products in front of engaged niche audiences; employee advocacy turns staff expertise and networks into reach, credibility and recruiting power. Both fail when brands script everything, pick partners by follower count alone, skip disclosure or pressure employees to post.

This skill builds organic creator partnership and employee advocacy programmes: choosing creators, briefs and rights, disclosure and compliance, gifting and paid deals, employee programmes, training, guidelines, content kits, recognition and measurement. Paid amplification of creator content (whitelisting and Spark Ads) sits in the Performance Marketing skills.

## Operating principles
1. **Fit over follower count.** Audience match, engagement quality and credibility matter more than size.
2. **Brief the idea, not the script.** Creators and employees know how to talk to their audiences.
3. **Disclose every partnership.** Regulators such as the US FTC and UK ASA and CMA require clear disclosure of paid or gifted content. [Confirmed, regulator guidance]
4. **Voluntary, supported advocacy.** Employees share because it helps them, not because they're told to.
5. **Measure outcomes.** Reach, engagement quality, traffic, sales, applications and content reuse.

## Creator tiers
| Tier | Typical audience | Strengths | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nano | Under about 10,000 | High trust, niche, affordable | Small reach; more management |
| Micro | About 10,000 to 100,000 | Engaged niche audiences | Varies widely in quality |
| Mid and macro | 100,000 to 1 million | Reach with some niche focus | Higher cost |
| Mega and celebrity | Over 1 million | Mass awareness | Expensive; weaker fit and trust |
Tiers are approximate; audience quality matters more than size.

## When to use, and when not to
Use it to reach new niche audiences, generate authentic content, support launches, build employer brand, extend executive and expert reach and create social proof.

Don't use it to buy fake engagement, hide sponsorships or force employees to post. Paid creator ads belong in the Performance Marketing skills.

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Goals (awareness, sales, content, recruiting, reputation).
2. Audience and platforms.
3. Budget for creators (gifting, fees, affiliate commissions).
4. Product availability and claims rules.
5. Employees interested in posting and their expertise.
6. Legal and HR requirements (disclosure, social media policy).

## Workflow: creators

### Step 1. Define goals and audience
Who you need to reach, on which platforms.

### Step 2. Find creators
Listening for people already mentioning you, platform search, creator marketplaces and customer lists.

### Step 3. Vet
Audience location and demographics, engagement quality (real comments), brand safety, past partnerships and content quality.

### Step 4. Brief
Goal, key message, must-include and must-avoid points, disclosure requirements, deliverables, timeline, usage rights; creative freedom on delivery.

### Step 5. Agree terms
Gifting, fee, affiliate or a mix; content rights and whitelisting permissions; exclusivity if needed.

### Step 6. Review and publish
Check facts and disclosure, not style; share and engage with the content.

### Step 7. Reuse with permission
Repost, use on website or in ads under agreed rights.

### Step 8. Measure and nurture
Results per creator; build long-term relationships with the best.

## Workflow: employee advocacy

### Step 1. Set purpose
Employer brand, expertise, customer trust, sales conversations.

### Step 2. Write guidelines
What to share, what to avoid (confidential information, client details), disclosure of employment, how to handle negative comments.

### Step 3. Recruit volunteers
Start with enthusiastic experts, founders and customer-facing staff.

### Step 4. Train
Profile optimisation, writing posts, finding ideas, engaging well.

### Step 5. Provide content kits
Suggested posts they can edit, visuals, company news and resources.

### Step 6. Recognise
Celebrate contributors; share results; never make posting mandatory.

### Step 7. Measure
Participation, reach, engagement, applications, inbound leads and sales conversations.

## Creator brief template
```
Brand and product:
Goal of the partnership:
Audience we want to reach:
Key message (one sentence):
Must include: product name, one key benefit, link or code, disclosure
Must avoid: medical claims, competitor mentions, etc.
Deliverables and platforms:
Timeline and review process:
Payment and usage rights:
Creative freedom: your style, your words
```

## Creator vetting checklist
- Audience location, age and interests match yours (ask for screenshots of analytics).
- Comments are real conversations, not generic emojis or bots.
- Engagement is consistent across posts, not inflated on a few.
- Content quality and tone fit the brand.
- No brand safety concerns in recent posts.
- Past partnerships disclosed properly.
- Rates and terms are reasonable for results.

## Disclosure basics
- Use platform paid partnership labels where available.
- Make disclosure clear and early ("Ad", "Paid partnership", "Gifted").
- Don't hide disclosure in hashtags at the end of long captions.
- Apply the same rules to employees posting about their employer's products where relevant.

## Employee guidelines (summary)
- Share your own experience and expertise; you speak for yourself.
- Disclose that you work for the company when discussing its products.
- Never share confidential information, client details or unreleased plans.
- Be respectful; don't argue in comments; escalate issues to the social team.
- Ask if unsure.

## Measurement
| Programme | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Creators | Reach, engagement quality, clicks, codes or affiliate sales, content reused |
| Employees | Participants, posts, reach among target roles, profile views, applications, leads |
Compare cost per result across creators and keep investing in the best long-term partners.

## Long-term creator relationships
One-off posts rarely change how an audience sees a brand. Ongoing partnerships, such as quarterly content, product feedback sessions, early access and ambassador roles, build familiarity and trust with the creator's audience, and usually cost less per result over time. Review each partner every quarter against agreed goals.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Creator posts without disclosure | Ask them to edit immediately; stop the partnership if repeated |
| Regulated products | Approved claims only; check platform and advertising rules |
| Employee posts something problematic | Follow the policy calmly; coach privately |
| Few employees willing | Start with two or three champions; show their results |
| B2B creators | Industry experts, analysts and practitioners with niche audiences |

## Worked example
A B2B HR software company's brand page reached few HR leaders. It recruited eight employees (two founders, HR experts and customer success managers) into an advocacy programme with training, monthly content kits and recognition, and partnered with five HR creators on LinkedIn and YouTube with clear disclosure and creative freedom. Employee posts reached far more target-role viewers than the brand page, creator videos produced a steady flow of demo requests, and job applications citing LinkedIn rose.

## Common mistakes
- Choosing creators by follower count alone.
- Over-scripting content.
- Missing disclosure.
- Mandatory employee posting.
- No content rights agreed for reuse.

## Questions to ask before you start
- Which creators does your audience already trust?
- Which employees want to build a professional presence?
- What disclosure and legal rules apply?

## Self-check before you answer
- Are creators vetted for fit and engagement quality?
- Do briefs give creative freedom with clear must-haves?
- Is disclosure required and checked?
- Is employee advocacy voluntary, supported and measured?
