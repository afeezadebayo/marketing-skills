# International Content Strategy

The default international content strategy is "translate everything". It is rarely the right one. Some content travels well (product pages, core guides); some needs adapting (pricing, regulation, examples); some only makes sense in one market (local laws, local events, local comparisons); and some markets need content that doesn't exist in the home language at all. Deciding what to translate, adapt, create or skip for each market is where international organic growth is won or lost.

This skill plans content per market: a global core, local adaptations, market-only content, publishing priorities, ownership and maintenance, so every market gets what its searchers need without wasting budget on pages nobody wants.

## Operating principles
1. **Demand decides.** Each market's keyword research and search results determine what to publish.
2. **Global core, local edge.** Share what travels; adapt or create what doesn't.
3. **Local expertise builds trust.** Local authors, examples and data matter for experience and trust.
4. **Maintain what you publish.** Every localised page needs updates when the original changes.
5. **Fewer, better pages.** Skip low-value translations; quality signals apply per site.

## Content decision matrix
| Content type | Decision | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product and feature pages | Translate and localise | Feature pages with local pricing and examples |
| Evergreen guides with universal advice | Translate with native editing | "How to write a project plan" |
| Regulated topics | Create locally with experts | Tax, employment, data protection guides |
| Market-specific needs | Create new | "CFDI e-invoicing guide" for Mexico |
| Comparisons and alternatives | Create per market | Local competitors differ |
| News and events | Local only | Local conferences and updates |
| Low-traffic or outdated content | Skip | Old blog posts with no demand |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when launching markets, when localised content underperforms, when planning annual content for several markets and when deciding what to translate.

Don't use it for translation workflows (`localisation-translation`) or single-market topical maps (the Content SEO `topical-map` skill).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Markets and priorities.
2. Existing content inventory with performance by market.
3. Keyword research per market.
4. Local expertise available (staff, partners, freelancers).
5. Budget and production capacity per market.
6. How updates to source content are managed today.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Inventory and score source content
Traffic, conversions and topic relevance per page; flag pages with international potential.

### Step 2. Map demand per market
Use multilingual research to list each market's priority topics.

### Step 3. Decide per page and topic
Translate, adapt, create or skip, using the matrix.

### Step 4. Find market-only gaps
Topics that matter locally but have no source page (local laws, local competitors, local use cases).

### Step 5. Assign ownership
Who writes, reviews and maintains each market's content; local experts for regulated topics.

### Step 6. Sequence publishing
Priority pages first (commercial, high-demand), then supporting content.

### Step 7. Plan maintenance
When source pages change, trigger updates in every localised version; review localised pages on a schedule.

### Step 8. Measure per market
Impressions, clicks, conversions and content gaps by market; adjust the plan quarterly.

## Per-market content plan template
```
| Market | Topic | Decision (translate/adapt/create/skip) | Target term | Owner | Reviewer | Priority | Publish by | Maintenance trigger |
```

## Maintenance triggers
| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Source page updated substantially | Queue updates in every localised version within an agreed time |
| Price, product or legal change | Update all markets on the same day |
| Localised page losing clicks | Review against local results and competitors |
| New local regulation | Update or create the local guide with an expert |
| Annual review date | Check accuracy, examples and links per market |

## Shared language, several countries
When one language serves several countries (Spanish for Spain and Latin America, French for France, Belgium, Switzerland and Canada), plan a shared core and decide country sections deliberately:
- **Shared:** product explanations, evergreen how-to guides, brand story.
- **Per country:** prices, currency, taxes, delivery, legal pages, regulations and local examples.
- **Market-only:** topics driven by local rules or culture, such as e-invoicing rules or local holidays.
This keeps production efficient while giving each country the details its searchers expect.

## Local authority and trust
Local authors or reviewers, local customer stories, local data and recognisable local partners make content more credible to readers and fit the experience and trust qualities search engines look for. A translated page signed by a headquarters author rarely earns the same trust as one reviewed or written by someone in the market.

## Prioritising topics per market
| Priority | Content | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Commercial pages (home, product, pricing, comparison) | Direct revenue and conversions |
| 2 | High-demand local topics with no source page | Biggest gap against local competitors |
| 3 | Evergreen guides with proven demand | Traffic and authority |
| 4 | Supporting articles and long tail | Breadth once the core performs |

## Content gap analysis against local competitors
1. List the top five local competitors for core terms in the market.
2. Export their ranking pages and topics.
3. Compare with your localised pages.
4. Mark topics they cover that you don't, and topics only relevant locally.
5. Prioritise by demand and business value.

## Measuring content per market
- Impressions and clicks for each market's pages from that country.
- Share of organic entrances from market-only content versus translated content.
- Conversions per page type and market.
- Pages with no impressions after six months (candidates to improve or remove).

## Working with local experts
For regulated or culturally sensitive topics, brief local experts with the outline, the target terms and the claims rules, then have them review or co-author the page. Show their name, role and credentials on the page where appropriate. Budget for an annual review by the same expert so advice stays current as local rules change.

## Avoiding duplicate effort
Keep one content calendar across markets so a guide created for one market can be adapted for others with similar needs, and so market teams don't commission overlapping pieces.

Review the plan every quarter with each market's results.

Retire pages that never earn impressions.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Small market, small budget | Translate the commercial core only |
| Strong local competitors with better content | Create market-specific angles rather than translating |
| Several countries share a language | Shared core with country-specific sections for prices and laws |
| Source content changes often | Use translation memory and update triggers |
| No local experts | Partner with local professionals for regulated topics |

## Worked example
An HR software company planned to translate its 400-article blog into German. Research showed German demand centred on employment law, working time recording and local payroll topics the English blog barely covered, while many English articles had no German demand. The plan translated 60 product and evergreen pages, adapted 40 guides with German legal context reviewed by an employment lawyer, created 25 Germany-only guides on working time recording and local regulations, and skipped the rest. The Germany-only guides became the top organic entry pages within two quarters.

## Common mistakes
- Translating everything by default.
- Ignoring topics that matter only locally.
- No local experts on regulated content.
- Publishing localised pages and never updating them.
- Measuring content success globally rather than per market.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- What do searchers in each market need that the home site doesn't cover?
- Which source content has international demand?
- Who will maintain localised content?

## Self-check before you answer
- Is every decision based on market demand?
- Are market-only gaps identified?
- Are owners, reviewers and maintenance triggers set?
- Is performance measured per market?
