# Pricing Page Optimisation

The pricing page is one of the most visited pages on most SaaS and subscription sites, and one of the most decisive. Visitors arrive with a budget in mind and a question: which plan fits me, what will I actually pay and is it worth it? Pages that make them work out the answer (confusing plan names, long feature tables, hidden limits, missing FAQs) lose buyers who were ready to start. Optimising the page doesn't change the prices themselves; it changes how easily visitors can understand them and choose.

This skill audits and improves pricing pages: plan presentation, recommended plans, value metrics and limits, comparison tables, billing options, objection handling, enterprise paths and calls to action, with research and tests.

## Operating principles
1. **Help visitors self-select.** Each plan should say who it's for.
2. **Make the cost predictable.** Show what customers will actually pay, including usage examples and taxes where relevant.
3. **Highlight one plan.** A recommended plan reduces decision effort for the main segment.
4. **Answer objections on the page.** Billing, cancellation, upgrades, security and support.
5. **Change presentation before price.** Pricing strategy belongs to product marketing; this skill improves clarity and choice.

## Pricing page elements
| Element | Good practice |
|---|---|
| Plan names | Describe who it's for (Starter, Team, Business) |
| One-line plan descriptions | "For freelancers getting started" |
| Price display | Clear currency, period and whether tax is included |
| Billing toggle | Monthly and annual, with the saving shown plainly |
| Recommended plan | Highlighted, matched to the main segment |
| Value metric and limits | Plain language with an example ("Up to 5 users", "10,000 emails a month") |
| Key features per plan | Five to eight outcomes, not a full feature dump |
| Comparison table | Grouped by outcome, collapsible, below the plan cards |
| Free trial or free plan | Clear terms, card required or not |
| Enterprise option | What's included and how to talk to sales |
| FAQs | Billing, cancellation, upgrades, discounts, security |
| Proof | Logos, ratings or a short quote near the plans |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when pricing page conversion is low, when visitors hesitate between plans, after packaging changes, before launches and when sales hears confusion about plans.

Don't use it to set prices or design packaging (the Product Marketing `pricing-packaging` skill) or for checkout steps after plan selection (`checkout-optimisation`).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Pricing page URL and current plans.
2. Conversion from pricing page to trial, sign-up or sales contact, by device and source.
3. Plan selection distribution and upgrade data.
4. Heatmaps, recordings or survey responses from the page.
5. Common pre-sale questions from sales and support.
6. Weekly traffic for test planning.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Measure
Pricing page conversion, plan clicks by plan, scroll depth, exits and where visitors go next (often FAQs, features or back to the homepage).

### Step 2. Research hesitation
Exit poll ("What, if anything, is stopping you from choosing a plan today?"), recordings, sales and support questions.

### Step 3. Audit plan clarity
Can a visitor match themselves to a plan in seconds? Are limits clear? Is the recommended plan right for the main segment?

### Step 4. Audit cost clarity
Currency, tax, billing period, usage examples and any add-ons visible before sign-up.

### Step 5. Audit objections
Are the top questions answered on the page?

### Step 6. Audit the enterprise path
Is it clear when to talk to sales and what happens next?

### Step 7. Check mobile
Plan cards stack sensibly; the comparison table is readable; the recommended plan stays visible.

### Step 8. Write hypotheses and test
Prioritise clarity changes; test plan presentation, recommendation and FAQs where traffic allows.

## Test ideas
| Problem | Hypothesis idea |
|---|---|
| Visitors unsure which plan fits | Add a team-size or use-case selector that recommends a plan |
| Annual option ignored | Show monthly equivalent and saving clearly; default to annual where appropriate and transparent |
| Usage pricing feels risky | Add a calculator and example bills; mention spending caps |
| Long comparison table overwhelms | Collapse into outcome groups below plan cards |
| Enterprise buyers bounce | Clear enterprise card listing security, support and procurement help |
| Many FAQ clicks | Move top answers into plan cards |

## Exit poll and research questions
| Placement | Question |
|---|---|
| Exit intent | What, if anything, is stopping you from choosing a plan today? |
| After plan click | What made you choose this plan? |
| Sales calls | Which plan questions do prospects ask most? |
| Support tickets | Which billing or limit questions come up after purchase? |

## Plan recommendation selector
Ask one or two questions (team size, main use case) and highlight the matching plan with a short reason: "For a team of 12 managing client projects, Team fits best because it includes guest access and time tracking." Keep all plans visible so visitors can still compare.

## FAQ topics that usually belong on the page
- Can I change plans later, and how is billing adjusted?
- What happens when I reach a limit?
- Is there a free trial, and do I need a card?
- How do cancellations and refunds work?
- Do prices include tax?
- What security and compliance standards do you meet?
- Do you offer discounts for annual billing, non-profits or education?

## Metrics to track
| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Pricing page to trial or sign-up rate | Main outcome |
| Plan click distribution | Whether the recommendation works |
| Annual versus monthly share | Billing presentation effect |
| Contact sales clicks | Enterprise path clarity |
| Refunds and downgrades in the first month | Whether visitors chose the right plan |

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Prices not shown (contact sales) | Consider showing starting prices or ranges; test if possible |
| Many plans | Group into three main plans plus enterprise; move niche options to add-ons |
| Several currencies | Show local currency based on choice, with clear switching |
| Usage-based pricing | Calculator, examples and caps |
| Discounts and promotions | Show them clearly and truthfully; avoid fake urgency |

## Worked example
A project management SaaS pricing page showed four plans with abstract names and a 60-row feature table. Exit polls showed visitors unsure which plan fitted their team size and what counted as a user. The new page renamed plans by team type, added one-line descriptions, highlighted the Team plan, rewrote limits with examples ("Guests are free and unlimited"), added a team-size selector and moved the six most-asked FAQs next to the plans. Trial starts from the pricing page rose, and sales reported fewer pre-sale questions about plans.

## Common mistakes
- Abstract plan names.
- Hidden limits and surprise costs.
- Feature tables instead of outcomes.
- No recommended plan.
- Fake discounts or urgency.

## Questions to ask before you change the page
- Which plan should most visitors choose, and why?
- What do visitors ask before buying?
- What will customers actually pay, including tax and usage?

## Self-check before you answer
- Can visitors match themselves to a plan quickly?
- Is the real cost clear before sign-up?
- Are top objections answered on the page?
- Are price decisions left to pricing strategy work?
