# Social Media Content Calendar

Social teams juggle several platforms, voices, formats, campaigns, launches, holidays and reactive moments, often with small teams. Without a calendar, posting becomes last-minute, approvals rush through, platforms get the same content pasted everywhere and good ideas are lost. A good calendar turns strategy into a weekly rhythm: pillars balanced, formats varied, people coordinated, assets ready on time and room left for timely posts.

This skill builds social media content calendars and production workflows: pillar mix, platform-specific planning, recurring series, campaign and launch slots, reactive capacity, batching, approval flows, scheduling tools and post-performance review.

## Operating principles
1. **Plan the rhythm, leave room for the moment.** Schedule core content and keep capacity for trends and news.
2. **Balance pillars.** No single theme or promotion should dominate the feed.
3. **Native per platform.** The same idea becomes different posts on each network.
4. **Batch production.** Filming and writing in batches saves time and improves quality.
5. **Review and adjust weekly.** Performance should shape next week's plan.

## Calendar structure
| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Date and time | Tuesday 09:00 |
| Platform | LinkedIn |
| Voice | Founder |
| Pillar | Month-end tips |
| Format | Carousel |
| Hook or title | "5 checks that catch 80% of month-end errors" |
| Copy status | Draft, approved |
| Asset status | Designed, approved |
| Campaign or series | "Close faster" series |
| Link and tracking | URL with UTM parameters |
| Owner | Social lead |
| Results (after posting) | Reach, engagement, clicks |

## Pillar mix example
| Pillar | Share of posts | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Education | 40% | Tips, how-tos, explainers |
| Community and customers | 20% | Customer stories, user content, spotlights |
| Behind the scenes and culture | 15% | Team, process, events |
| Industry and opinion | 15% | Takes on news, trends |
| Promotion | 10% | Launches, offers, events |
Adjust to goals and platform; promotion lands better when the rest of the feed earns attention.

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when posting is last-minute or inconsistent, when several people post, when coordinating launches across platforms and when moving from reactive posting to a plan.

Don't use it to choose platforms or goals (`social-media-strategy`) or for the whole marketing editorial calendar (the Content, Copy and PR `editorial-operations` skill).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Social strategy: goals, platforms, pillars, voices.
2. Key dates: launches, campaigns, events, holidays, industry moments.
3. Team, roles and approval requirements.
4. Production capacity (design, video, writing).
5. Scheduling and project tools.
6. Recent performance data.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Set weekly rhythm per platform
For example LinkedIn: founder three times, brand page three times; Instagram: four Reels, two carousels, daily Stories.

### Step 2. Add recurring series
Weekly tip, Friday customer spotlight, monthly live Q&A; series simplify planning and build habits.

### Step 3. Block key dates
Launches, campaigns, events and relevant awareness days that fit the brand.

### Step 4. Fill with pillar-balanced ideas
From an idea bank fed by customer questions, comments, sales, support and trends.

### Step 5. Adapt per platform
One idea becomes a LinkedIn carousel, an Instagram Reel and an X thread, each native.

### Step 6. Batch production
Write a week or two at a time; film several videos in one session.

### Step 7. Approve and schedule
Clear approvers; scheduling tools for planned posts; manual posting where platforms favour it.

### Step 8. Keep reactive capacity
Reserve time each week for trends, news and community responses.

### Step 9. Review weekly
What performed, what didn't, what to change next week.

## Weekly planning meeting (30 minutes)
1. Review last week's top and bottom posts (10 minutes).
2. Confirm next week's calendar and gaps (10 minutes).
3. Assign production and approvals (5 minutes).
4. Note reactive opportunities and risks (5 minutes).

## Approval tiers
| Content | Approval |
|---|---|
| Routine posts within guidelines | Social lead |
| Product claims, pricing, partnerships | Product marketing or legal |
| Executive posts | The executive (or delegate) |
| Sensitive topics, crises | Communications lead and leadership |

## Idea bank sources
| Source | Example ideas |
|---|---|
| Customer questions and support tickets | How-to posts, myth-busting, FAQs on video |
| Comments on past posts | Follow-up posts, video replies |
| Sales calls | Objection-handling posts, comparisons |
| Product updates | Demos, tips, behind the scenes |
| Team and culture | Day-in-the-life, events, milestones |
| Industry news and trends | Timely takes, trend adaptations |
| Long-form content | Clips, carousels and threads from reports, webinars and blogs |
Tag ideas by pillar and platform so planners can fill gaps quickly.

## One idea, several native posts
| Platform | Version |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Carousel with five steps and a short founder story |
| Instagram | 30-second Reel showing the result first |
| TikTok | Relatable POV video with keyword caption |
| X | Short thread with a chart |
| YouTube | Full tutorial plus a Short |

## Batching day plan
- Morning: film four to six short videos with prepared scripts and hooks.
- Midday: capture B-roll, photos and behind-the-scenes clips.
- Afternoon: record one longer video or live session.
- Next day: edit, write captions and schedule.

## Scheduling tips
- Schedule planned posts in a tool, but post natively where features (trending audio, collab posts) require it.
- Use UTM parameters on every link.
- Check scheduled posts against news before they go out.
- Keep a pause procedure for crises.

## Weekly review metrics
Top and bottom posts by engagement rate, saves, shares and clicks; best formats and hooks; any comment themes worth turning into posts; and progress on series consistency.

## Awareness days
Use only the awareness days and holidays that fit the brand and audience; generic posts for every international day add noise without value.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| One-person team | Fewer platforms, series formats, heavy batching |
| Many stakeholders | Approval tiers and deadlines; a request form |
| Global accounts | Time zones and local holidays; regional calendars |
| Regulated industry | Compliance review lead times built in |
| Crisis or sensitive news | Pause scheduled posts; review before resuming |

## Worked example
A consumer electronics brand's two-person social team posted reactively and often missed launches. The new calendar set a weekly rhythm per platform, three recurring series (Tech Tip Tuesday, Customer Setup Friday, monthly live Q&A), blocked launch windows with assets due two weeks ahead, batched filming into one day every fortnight and reserved two slots a week for trends. Weekly 30-minute reviews shifted effort towards the best-performing formats. Posting became consistent, launches were fully supported and engagement rose steadily.

## Common mistakes
- Posting the same content everywhere.
- Too much promotion.
- No room for reactive posts.
- Last-minute approvals.
- Never reviewing what worked.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- What rhythm can the team sustain for a year?
- Which dates and launches must be supported?
- Who approves what, and how fast?

## Self-check before you answer
- Is there a sustainable weekly rhythm per platform?
- Are pillars balanced and series defined?
- Is reactive capacity reserved?
- Is there a weekly performance review?
