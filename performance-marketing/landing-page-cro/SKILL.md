# Landing Pages and Conversion Rate Optimisation for Paid Traffic

Paid media buys the visit; the landing page decides whether it becomes a customer. Doubling conversion rate halves cost per acquisition without touching bids, which makes landing pages one of the cheapest growth levers in paid media. Yet many accounts send expensive clicks to a generic homepage, a slow mobile page or a form with twelve fields.

This skill audits and improves landing pages for paid traffic: message match with ads and queries, clarity, proof, friction, speed, mobile experience and tracking, then runs tests that measure real business outcomes.

## Operating principles
1. **Message match.** The page continues the promise the ad made, in the same words.
2. **One page, one goal.** Remove distractions that don't help the visitor decide.
3. **Proof beats claims.** Reviews, numbers, logos, guarantees and demonstrations carry the argument.
4. **Speed and mobile first.** Most paid social traffic is mobile and impatient.
5. **Test on outcomes.** Purchases, qualified leads and revenue per visitor, not just clicks on a button.

## Landing page checklist
| Area | Check |
|---|---|
| Headline | Matches the ad and query; states the outcome |
| Above the fold | Value, proof and call to action visible on mobile without scrolling |
| Offer | Clear price, plan or next step; no surprises later |
| Proof | Reviews, ratings, case studies, numbers with sources, recognisable logos |
| Objections | FAQs, guarantees, delivery and returns, security |
| Form or checkout | Fewest fields needed; guest checkout; payment options; autofill |
| Speed | Fast LCP on mobile; no layout jumps; light scripts |
| Trust | Contact details, policies, secure checkout |
| Tracking | Conversion events fire once; values passed; consent respected |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when conversion rates are low or falling, when CPA rises while CTR holds, when launching campaigns, when ads send traffic to the homepage, and to plan a testing programme.

Don't use it for organic SEO landing pages (use the Content SEO skills) or for ad creative (`ad-creative-testing`).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Landing page URLs and the campaigns, ads and queries sending traffic.
2. Conversion rates by page, device and source for 90 days.
3. Analytics funnels, heatmaps or session recordings if available.
4. Page speed data (PageSpeed Insights, Core Web Vitals field data).
5. Customer research: objections, questions, reviews.
6. Traffic volume per page for test planning.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Map traffic to pages
Which campaigns and audiences land where? Find mismatches: specific ads pointing to general pages.

### Step 2. Find the leaks
Funnel drop-off by step and device; heatmaps and recordings for confusion; form analytics for abandoned fields.

### Step 3. Review message match
Compare the ad headline and promise with the page headline and first screen.

### Step 4. Review clarity and proof
Can a visitor understand what it is, who it's for, why it's better and what to do next within five seconds?

### Step 5. Remove friction
Fewer form fields, guest checkout, clear pricing, fewer steps, payment options people use in that market.

### Step 6. Fix speed and mobile
Compress images, cut heavy scripts, avoid layout shifts, make buttons easy to tap.

### Step 7. Prioritise tests
Score ideas by expected impact, confidence and effort; test the biggest changes first.

### Step 8. Test and learn
A/B tests with enough traffic, judged on purchases or qualified leads and revenue per visitor; log every result.

## Test ideas by problem
| Problem | Test |
|---|---|
| High bounce from paid social | Headline matching the ad, product shown in use above the fold |
| Low form completion | Fewer fields, multi-step form with the easiest question first |
| Cart abandonment | Show total cost early, guest checkout, express payments |
| Low trust | Reviews near the call to action, guarantees, recognisable logos |
| Confused visitors | Simpler page with one offer instead of several |

## Sample size and duration
Low-traffic pages can't support many small tests. Use bigger changes (new page versus old), longer tests, or pre-post comparisons with care. As a guide, test until you have enough conversions in each variant to detect the change you care about and at least one or two full weekly cycles.

## Prioritising test ideas (ICE)
| Idea | Impact (1-10) | Confidence (1-10) | Ease (1-10) | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three-field first step instead of 14-field form | 9 | 7 | 6 | 22 |
| Reviews beside the call to action | 5 | 6 | 9 | 20 |
| New hero image | 3 | 4 | 9 | 16 |
Sum or multiply the scores consistently, and revisit confidence after each test.

## Five-second test
Show the page to someone unfamiliar with the business for five seconds, then ask:
1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What would you do next?
If answers vary, the headline and first screen need work before any other test.

## Form design for lead quality
- Start with the easiest question (postcode, company size) to build momentum.
- Ask qualifying questions that help sales, not curiosity questions.
- Use multi-step forms when more than five fields are needed.
- Show progress and what happens next.
- Validate fields as people type to avoid errors at submit.
- Judge changes on qualified leads, not just submissions.

## Templates for many campaigns
| Template | Use | Key elements |
|---|---|---|
| Product page variant | Shopping and product ads | Product focus, reviews, delivery, add to cart |
| Offer page | Promotions and seasonal campaigns | Offer first, deadline (if real), proof |
| Problem-solution page | Prospecting social traffic | Problem, how it works, proof, offer |
| Comparison page | Competitor and "alternative" searches | Honest comparison table, switching help |
| Lead gen page | B2B and services | Outcome headline, proof, short form, next steps |

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Many campaigns, few pages | Build templates per intent or segment rather than one page per ad |
| Lead generation | Balance form fields between volume and quality; judge on qualified leads |
| Marketplace or platform listings | Optimise the listing (images, reviews, price) as the landing page |
| Legal or regulatory content | Keep required information while simplifying layout |
| Page builders slow the page | Strip unused widgets and scripts or build lighter templates |

## Worked example
A home insurance broker sent Google and Meta traffic to a long homepage with a 14-field quote form. Mobile conversion was a third of desktop. The new landing pages matched ad promises by product, showed a three-field first step (postcode, property type, email), moved reviews and a price guarantee next to the button and cut page weight by half. Tested against the old page with qualified quotes as the metric, the new page lifted qualified quote rate substantially on mobile, lowering cost per qualified quote across both platforms.

## Common mistakes
- Sending paid traffic to the homepage.
- Testing tiny changes on low-traffic pages.
- Judging tests on clicks rather than purchases or qualified leads.
- Slow, script-heavy mobile pages.
- Hiding costs until checkout.

## Questions to ask before you change pages
- Which ads and queries send traffic to each page?
- Where do mobile visitors drop off?
- How much traffic and how many conversions does each page get per week?

## Self-check before you answer
- Does each page match the ad and query that brings visitors?
- Are proof, objections and friction addressed?
- Is speed checked on mobile with field data?
- Are tests sized and judged on business outcomes?
