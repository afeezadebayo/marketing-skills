# Editorial Operations and Content Calendar

Good content strategies often fail in execution. Ideas sit in spreadsheets, briefs are vague, drafts wait weeks for expert review, approvals bounce between five people, nobody knows what publishes next week and distribution is an afterthought. Editorial operations turn strategy into a reliable flow: ideas become briefs, briefs become drafts, drafts get reviewed and published on schedule, and every piece is distributed and measured.

This skill sets up editorial operations: roles, workflow stages, briefs, calendars, review and approval rules, subject matter expert input, AI tool policies, quality checks, publishing and distribution checklists, and capacity planning.

## Operating principles
1. **One calendar, one source of truth.** Everyone sees what's planned, in progress and published.
2. **Clear roles.** Each stage has one owner; approvers are few and named.
3. **Briefs before drafts.** A good brief saves more time than any editing.
4. **Protect expert time.** Short interviews and focused reviews beat long document comments.
5. **Distribution is part of the workflow.** A piece isn't done when it's published.

## Workflow stages
| Stage | Owner | Output | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea | Anyone | Idea with audience, goal and pillar | Ongoing |
| Prioritised | Editor | Accepted into calendar | Weekly planning |
| Brief | Editor or strategist | Brief with angle, audience, sources, outline | 1 to 2 days |
| Research and interview | Writer | Notes, quotes, data | 2 to 5 days |
| Draft | Writer | First draft | 3 to 7 days |
| Edit | Editor | Structural and line edit | 1 to 3 days |
| Expert and legal review | Named reviewers | Approved facts and claims | 2 to 3 days |
| Design and assets | Designer | Images, charts, social assets | 1 to 3 days |
| Publish | Editor | Live piece with metadata | 1 day |
| Distribute | Marketing | Newsletter, social, sales, partners | Launch week |
| Measure and update | Editor | Results and refresh date | 30, 90 days |

## Roles
| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Managing editor | Calendar, priorities, standards, final approval |
| Writer | Research, interviews, drafts |
| Subject matter expert | Insight, accuracy |
| Designer | Visuals and assets |
| Legal or compliance (when needed) | Claims and regulated content |
| Distribution owner | Promotion plan and execution |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when content is late, inconsistent or stuck in review, when scaling a content team or agency relationship, when introducing AI writing tools and when multiple teams publish.

Don't use it to set strategy (`content-strategy`) or for detailed SEO briefs (the Content SEO `content-brief` skill).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Content strategy and target cadence.
2. Team members, freelancers and agencies.
3. Current workflow, tools (project management, docs, CMS) and pain points.
4. Review and approval requirements.
5. Policies on AI writing tools, if any.
6. Distribution channels.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Map the current flow
Where ideas come from, who touches each piece, where it waits.

### Step 2. Define stages and owners
Use the table above; remove stages that add no value.

### Step 3. Standardise briefs
One template for all writers and agencies.

### Step 4. Set review rules
Who reviews what, deadlines for each review, how disagreements are resolved.

### Step 5. Build the calendar
Planned publish dates, owners, status, pillar, format and distribution channels.

### Step 6. Set AI tool policy
Where AI can help (research summaries, outlines, first drafts for low-risk pieces), where it can't, disclosure rules and mandatory human editing and fact-checking.

### Step 7. Add quality and publishing checklists
Facts, sources, voice, links, metadata, accessibility and images.

### Step 8. Plan capacity
Pieces per month per writer, expert hours, design time; buffer for urgent work.

### Step 9. Review monthly
Cycle times, missed dates, quality scores and results.

```
Title (working):

Goal of the piece (and metric):
Pillar and funnel stage:
Angle and point of view:
Key points and outline:

Examples and proof to include:
Voice notes:
Call to action:
Length and format:
Distribution plan:
Deadline and reviewers:
```

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Title | Month-end close checklist |
| Status | Draft |
| Owner | Writer name |
| Pillar | Faster close |
| Format | Guide plus template |
| Publish date | 14 November |
| Distribution | Newsletter, LinkedIn, sales enablement |
| Refresh date | May next year |

## Quality checklist before publishing
- Facts, numbers and quotes checked against sources.
- Claims approved by the right expert or compliance reviewer.
- Voice matches the brand guide; no generic or machine-sounding passages.
- Headings tell the story; paragraphs are short.
- Links work and point to the best internal and external sources.
- Images have alt text; charts are readable on mobile.
- Title, description and social preview are written.
- Author and reviewer named where relevant.

## Distribution checklist
- Newsletter slot booked.
- Social posts drafted for each channel.
- Sales and customer success told, with a one-line summary.
- Partners or communities identified.
- Repurposing plan agreed (clips, carousels, quotes).

## Monthly operations metrics
| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Cycle time from brief to publish | Speed |
| Share of pieces published on schedule | Reliability |
| Review time per stage | Bottlenecks |
| Quality score average | Standards |
| Results per piece at 90 days | Value |

Revisit stages and owners whenever the team, tools or cadence change.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Experts never have time | 30-minute recorded interviews; writer drafts from transcripts |
| Too many approvers | One accountable approver; others consulted only when needed |
| Agency writers | Same brief template and quality checklist; regular feedback |
| Urgent requests | Buffer capacity and a rule for what gets bumped |
| AI drafts flooding the calendar | Quality gate and human editing before scheduling |

## Worked example
A fintech content team missed half its planned publish dates. Mapping the flow showed drafts waited an average of 12 days for review by four approvers, briefs were a single line and nobody owned distribution. The new process introduced a standard brief, one accountable approver plus compliance review only for regulated claims, 30-minute expert interviews replacing written input, a shared calendar with status and a distribution owner. Average cycle time fell from five weeks to two, and every piece launched with a newsletter and social plan.

## Common mistakes
- Vague briefs.
- Too many approvers.
- No shared calendar.
- Distribution left until after publishing.
- Using AI drafts without human editing and fact-checking.

## Questions to ask before you design the workflow
- Where do pieces wait longest today?
- Who must approve what, and why?
- How will AI tools be used, and checked?

## Self-check before you answer
- Does every stage have one owner and a time expectation?
- Is there a standard brief?
- Are review rules and AI policy written down?
- Is distribution built into the workflow?
