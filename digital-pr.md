# Digital PR and Media Relations

Digital PR earns coverage, mentions and links from news sites, trade publications, blogs, newsletters and podcasts. Done well, it builds brand awareness and credibility, strengthens search visibility through authoritative links and shapes what AI answer engines say about a company. Done badly, it means mass-emailing press releases about minor updates to journalists who never cover the topic, and measuring success by how many emails went out.

This skill plans and runs digital PR: newsworthy angles, data and expert stories, reactive commentary, press releases that journalists can use, targeted media lists, pitches, embargoes, relationships and measurement of coverage quality.

## Operating principles
1. **News value first.** Journalists cover what their readers care about, not what the company wants to say.
2. **Target precisely.** A short list of the right journalists beats a large list of the wrong ones.
3. **Make journalists' jobs easier.** Data, experts, visuals and quotes ready to use.
4. **Build relationships.** Helpful, reliable sources get called again.
5. **Measure quality, not volume.** Relevant outlets, message pull-through, links and business impact.

## What makes a story newsworthy
| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| New data | "Small firms spend 6 hours a week chasing late invoices" |
| Timeliness | Commentary on a new regulation the day it's announced |
| Impact | How a change affects many people or businesses |
| Conflict or surprise | Findings that contradict common belief |
| Human interest | A customer story with real stakes |
| Local angle | Regional breakdowns of national data |
| Expertise | A credible expert explaining a complex issue |

## Tactics
| Tactic | Description | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Data-led campaigns | Original research or analysis with headline findings | High |
| Reactive commentary (newsjacking) | Expert comments on breaking news, sent quickly | Low to medium |
| Expert source requests | Responding to journalist requests (for example via source-request services) | Low |
| Press releases | Material news: funding, launches, partnerships, results | Medium |
| Thought leadership placements | Bylined articles and podcast interviews | Medium |
| Creative campaigns | Tools, visual stories or stunts tied to the brand | High |
| Local and trade PR | Regional and industry publications | Medium |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it to earn coverage and links, launch major news, build executive profiles, support research reports and improve brand visibility in search and AI answers.

Don't use it for paid placements presented as editorial (disclose sponsored content) or for crisis response in depth (bring in specialist crisis communications support).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Goals (awareness, links, credibility, launches) and target audiences.
2. Available stories: data, research, experts, customers, news.
3. Spokespeople and their expertise.
4. Target publications and past coverage.
5. Approval process and legal constraints.
6. Monitoring tools and backlink data.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Find angles
List stories by news value; prioritise data, timely commentary and human stories.

### Step 2. Prepare assets
Key findings, charts, quotes, expert availability, images and a short press release or summary.

### Step 3. Build targeted media lists
Journalists who covered similar stories recently; note their beats and preferred formats.

### Step 4. Write pitches
Short, personalised, with the story in the first line and why it matters to their readers.

### Step 5. Time outreach
Embargoes for data stories; speed for reactive commentary; avoid busy news days where possible.

### Step 6. Follow up once
A brief, useful follow-up; never repeated chasing.

### Step 7. Support coverage
Respond quickly with quotes, data and images; thank journalists; share coverage.

### Step 8. Measure
Coverage in target outlets, message pull-through, links and their quality, referral traffic, branded search and AI mentions.

## Pitch template
```
Subject: [Data or news hook in under 10 words]
Hi [first name],
I saw your piece on [recent related article]. New data from [company] shows [headline finding].
Key points:
- [Finding 1 with number]
- [Finding 2 with segment or regional angle]
[Expert name, role] is available for comment today.
Full data, method and charts: [link]
Thanks,
[Name, role, phone]
```

## Press release structure
```
Headline: what happened, in plain words
Subheadline: why it matters
First paragraph: who, what, when, where, why
Second paragraph: key details and numbers
Quote: from a spokesperson, saying something useful, not generic praise
Supporting details: context, availability, pricing
About the company: short boilerplate
Media contact
```

## Media list fields
| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Journalist and outlet | Business reporter, national daily |
| Beat | Small business, payments |
| Recent relevant article | Link and date |
| Preferred contact and format | Email; data with charts |
| Relationship notes | Quoted our CEO in March |
| Last contacted | Date and topic |
Keep lists small and current; remove contacts who change beats or ask not to be contacted.

## Reactive commentary process
1. Monitor news and journalist requests daily for topics where your experts can add value.
2. Pre-approve spokespeople and topics so comments go out within hours.
3. Write two or three sentences of genuine insight, not promotion.
4. Send quickly with the expert's credentials and availability.
5. Log responses and coverage for measurement.

## Coverage quality scoring
| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Does the audience match our customers? |
| Authority | Is the outlet respected in the field? |
| Message | Did key messages or data appear? |
| Link | Is there a followed link to a relevant page? |
| Spokesperson | Was our expert quoted by name? |
Score each piece of coverage and report the average, not just the count.

## Relationship habits
Share useful data even when it won't feature your company, reply quickly, keep promises on exclusives and embargoes, and thank journalists after coverage. Reliable sources get called first.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Minor product updates | Use owned channels; don't pitch media |
| Journalist asks for exclusives | Agree terms clearly; honour them |
| Negative coverage | Respond factually and quickly; correct errors politely |
| Paid placements | Label as sponsored and use qualified link attributes |
| Small brand, no news | Lead with data, experts and customer stories |

## Worked example
A fintech startup sent press releases about every feature update and earned almost no coverage. The new programme stopped routine releases, launched a quarterly data story using anonymised payment trends, set up rapid expert commentary on interest rate decisions and responded daily to journalist source requests. Within six months it earned coverage in national business media and trade publications, gained links from authoritative sites, and branded search rose.

## Common mistakes
- Pitching company news nobody cares about.
- Mass, untargeted email lists.
- Generic quotes in press releases.
- Repeated chasing of journalists.
- Measuring emails sent instead of coverage quality.

## Questions to ask before you pitch
- Why would this journalist's readers care?
- What data, expert or story makes it new?
- Which outlets matter most to our audience?

## Self-check before you answer
- Does each story have clear news value?
- Are media lists targeted and current?
- Are assets ready for journalists to use?
- Is success measured by coverage quality and impact?
