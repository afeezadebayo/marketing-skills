# Ad Creative Strategy and Testing

As platforms automate targeting and bidding, creative has become the biggest lever advertisers still control. The ad decides who stops scrolling, who the algorithm shows it to next, and whether the click turns into a customer. Most teams test small variations of one idea and wonder why results plateau. The teams that grow test distinct concepts, learn which angles work for which audiences and build a production system that keeps fresh, on-brand creative flowing.

This skill builds a creative strategy grounded in customer insight, plans concept tests across Meta, TikTok, YouTube, Google and LinkedIn, sets metrics for each stage of an ad, and turns results into a repeatable creative system.

## Operating principles
1. **Start with customers, not formats.** Reviews, sales calls, support tickets and surveys hold the angles that sell.
2. **Test concepts, then iterate.** Big differences first (angle, format, hook), small tweaks later.
3. **Measure each stage.** Thumb-stop and hold rates for attention, click-through for interest, conversion and CAC for business value.
4. **Diversity feeds automation.** Different concepts reach different people in automated delivery systems.
5. **Claims must be true.** Substantiate performance claims, show real results and follow platform and advertising rules.

## Where angles come from
| Source | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Customer reviews (yours and competitors') | Exact phrases about problems, outcomes and surprises |
| Sales and support calls | Objections, questions, moments of "that's what I need" |
| Surveys | Why they bought, what nearly stopped them, alternatives considered |
| Search terms | How people describe the problem |
| Comments on ads and posts | Doubts and enthusiasm in their words |
| Competitor ad libraries | Angles already saturated, gaps nobody uses |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when performance plateaus, when creative fatigue raises costs, when launching a product or market, when building a creative testing programme, and when briefing creators or agencies.

Don't use it for landing page optimisation (`landing-page-cro`) or for platform setup and bidding (the channel skills).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Product, audience segments and positioning.
2. Customer research sources: reviews, surveys, call notes, support tickets.
3. Current ads with performance by creative for 90 days.
4. Brand guidelines and claims that can or can't be made.
5. Production capacity: in-house, creators, agencies, budget.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Mine insights
Pull 50 to 100 customer phrases; group them into problems, desired outcomes, objections and proof points.

### Step 2. Write angles
Each angle is one reason to buy for one type of customer: "for busy parents, dinner in 15 minutes", "for sceptics, a dermatologist explains the ingredient".

### Step 3. Pick formats per platform
UGC and creator video, demos, testimonials, founder stories, statics, carousels, motion graphics; vertical for TikTok, Reels and Shorts.

### Step 4. Write hooks and scripts
Hook in the first 2 to 3 seconds (problem, result first, bold claim with proof, question). Body shows proof. End with a clear call to action.

### Step 5. Plan the test
A testing campaign or budget with several new concepts at once, judged on the stage metrics and CAC from your own data.

### Step 6. Read results by stage
| Stage | Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Thumb-stop rate (3-second views ÷ impressions), hook rate | Does the opening stop people? |
| Retention | Hold rate (views to 50% or 15 seconds ÷ 3-second views) | Is the story engaging? |
| Interest | Click-through rate | Does it make people want more? |
| Conversion | Conversion rate, CAC, new-customer share | Does it bring the right buyers? |

### Step 7. Iterate winners
New hooks, lengths, people and formats on winning angles; retire losers.

### Step 8. Build the library
Tag every ad by angle, format, hook, person and result, so the team learns which combinations work.

## Creative brief template
```
Product and audience segment:
Angle (one reason to buy):
Insight behind it (customer quote):
Format and platform:
Hook options (3):
Proof to show:
Call to action:
Mandatory claims and disclaimers; claims to avoid:
Length and specs:
Success metrics:
```

## Hook formulas
| Hook type | Example |
|---|---|
| Problem call-out | "If you dread 6pm because nobody knows what's for dinner..." |
| Result first | "This is what my skin looked like after 30 days" (with real, consented footage) |
| Myth-buster | "You don't need eight products for clear skin" |
| Question | "Still paying for three project tools?" |
| Demonstration | Product doing the job in the first second |
| Social proof | "Why 10,000 teachers switched this term" (only if true and sourced) |

## Concept test design
1. Pick 3 to 6 concepts that differ in angle or format.
2. Give each similar budget and time in a testing campaign.
3. Wait for enough conversions to compare (or use stage metrics early for clear losers).
4. Promote winners unedited to scaling campaigns.
5. Iterate winners on hook, length, person and format.
6. Tag everything in the library.

## Creative library tags
| Tag | Values |
|---|---|
| Angle | decision fatigue, food waste, family, price, quality |
| Format | UGC, demo, testimonial, founder, static, carousel |
| Hook type | problem, result, myth, question, demo, proof |
| Person | customer, creator, expert, founder, none |
| Length | under 15s, 15-30s, over 30s |
| Result | CAC, new-customer share, hold rate, CTR |
Review the library monthly to find patterns: which angles win for which segments, and how long winners last before fatigue.

## Fatigue signals
- Frequency rising while CTR and conversion rate fall for two weeks.
- Hook rate dropping on a previously strong video.
- CPA rising in the campaign without changes elsewhere.
Refresh with new hooks or formats on the same angle before retiring the angle entirely.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Tiny budget | Test fewer concepts at a time; use statics and simple UGC |
| Regulated claims (health, finance) | Substantiate claims, include required disclaimers, get approvals |
| Brand team resists UGC style | Test it alongside brand creative and show the data |
| Creative fatigue in days (TikTok) | Plan production for weekly refreshes |
| AI-generated images or video | Follow platform disclosure rules; don't use them to fake results or testimonials |

## Worked example
A meal-kit brand's Meta ads had used the same polished video for months. Mining 400 reviews surfaced three strong angles: no more "what's for dinner" decisions, less food waste and cooking with kids. The team produced 12 concepts across UGC, recipe demos and static benefit cards. Two angles (decision fatigue and cooking with kids) beat the old ad on CAC; UGC from parents performed best for the kids angle. Iterations with new hooks extended their life, and the library tags showed problem-first hooks consistently outperformed product-first hooks for this audience.

## Common mistakes
- Testing button colours while concepts never change.
- Judging creative only on CTR.
- No link between ad promise and landing page.
- Making claims you can't substantiate.
- No tagging, so lessons are lost.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- What do customers say in their own words about why they bought?
- Which claims can you prove?
- How many new concepts can you produce each week?

## Self-check before you answer
- Are angles grounded in customer language?
- Is the test plan concept-level first, with stage metrics?
- Are results judged on CAC from first-party data?
- Are claims substantiated and compliant?
