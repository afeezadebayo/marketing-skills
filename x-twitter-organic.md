# X (Twitter) Organic Growth

X remains a place for real-time conversation, news, technology, media, finance, developers and niche communities. It rewards speed, sharp ideas, clear opinions and active participation in conversations. It punishes brands that treat it as a link feed, ignore replies or post corporate announcements nobody asked for. Reach and features have changed often since 2023, including the role of paid verification, so tactics need regular testing rather than assumptions.

This skill builds organic X programmes for brands and individuals: profile setup, positioning, post types, threads, replies and quote posts, communities and Spaces, cadence, engagement and measurement. It also notes when similar text-first networks such as Threads, Bluesky or LinkedIn may suit the audience better.

## Operating principles
1. **Join conversations, don't just broadcast.** Replies and quote posts often build more audience than original posts.
2. **Be specific and opinionated.** Clear takes and useful details stand out in a fast feed.
3. **Speed matters for news.** Timely commentary earns attention; late commentary disappears.
4. **People outperform brands.** Founders, engineers and experts usually build audiences faster than brand handles.
5. **Test, because the platform changes.** Check what works for your audience regularly.

## Post types
| Type | Use | Tips |
|---|---|---|
| Single post | Ideas, takes, announcements | One clear point; strong first line |
| Thread | Step-by-step guides, stories, breakdowns | First post states the promise; number posts; end with a summary |
| Reply | Joining relevant conversations | Add value: data, experience, a counterpoint |
| Quote post | Commenting on others' posts | Add your angle; avoid pile-ons |
| Image or chart | Data, screenshots, before and after | Readable on mobile; alt text |
| Video | Demos, short explainers | Captions; hook in first seconds |
| Poll | Light research and engagement | Share results afterwards |
| Spaces (live audio) | Discussions, launches, Q&A | Promote in advance; invite guests |
| Communities | Niche groups around topics | Participate, don't only promote |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it for brands and people in tech, media, finance, developer tools, politics and fast-moving industries, for real-time support and for building personal expert audiences.

Don't use it as a link dump, for audiences who aren't on the platform, or for paid campaigns (the Performance Marketing skills).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Goals (audience, traffic, community, support, hiring).
2. Account or accounts and their analytics.
3. Audience and the accounts they follow.
4. Voices available and their expertise.
5. Topics and conversations relevant to the brand.
6. Time available daily.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Set up the profile
Clear name, bio stating what you post about and for whom, link, pinned post showing your best work.

### Step 2. Define positioning
Topics you'll own and the angle (practical, analytical, insider, humorous).

### Step 3. Map conversations
Lists of accounts, hashtags and communities where your audience talks.

### Step 4. Plan content mix
For example: original posts and threads, daily replies, weekly long-form or thread, occasional Spaces.

### Step 5. Write for the feed
Short, specific, scannable; numbers and examples; avoid corporate language.

### Step 6. Engage daily
Reply to mentions and relevant posts; thank people who share; join timely discussions.

### Step 7. Handle support and issues
Route customer complaints to support quickly and publicly acknowledge them where appropriate.

### Step 8. Measure and test
Impressions, engagement rate, replies, profile visits, follows, link clicks and conversions; test formats and times.

## Thread template
```
1/ Promise: "We analysed 200 SaaS pricing pages. Here are 7 patterns that convert."
2/ Context or method in one post.
3-8/ One pattern per post with an example or screenshot.
9/ Summary of the patterns.
10/ Call to action: follow, newsletter or resource.
```

## Reply guidelines
- Add new information, experience or a respectful counterpoint.
- Keep it short; one idea per reply.
- Avoid self-promotion unless directly relevant and helpful.
- Never reply in anger; step away from pile-ons.

## Daily routine (20 to 30 minutes)
1. Check mentions and replies; answer questions and route support issues.
2. Scan your lists of key accounts and communities for conversations to join.
3. Write three to five useful replies or quote posts.
4. Post one original idea, chart or short thread.
5. Note ideas from conversations for longer threads.

## Writing for the feed
| Weak | Stronger |
|---|---|
| We're excited to announce our new integration! | You can now sync invoices from Xero in one click. 2-minute demo below. |
| Check out our latest blog post | 3 reasons month-end runs late, from 120 finance teams we work with: |
| Thoughts on today's news? | The new rule mainly affects firms with 50+ staff. What changes for them: |

## Metrics
| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Engagement rate per post | Content quality |
| Replies and quote posts | Conversation |
| Profile visits and follows after posts | Audience building |
| Link clicks and conversions | Business value |
| Support response time | Customer experience |

## Spaces checklist
- Topic and guests confirmed a week ahead.
- Announced in posts and pinned.
- Co-host to manage speakers and comments.
- Recording clipped into short posts afterwards.

## Testing as the platform changes
Every quarter, compare formats (text, threads, images, video), posting times and reply habits over a few weeks each, and keep what lifts engagement among your target audience rather than relying on advice that may be out of date.

## Lists worth building
Keep private lists of customers, prospects, partners, journalists and peers in your field. Checking them daily surfaces the conversations where a useful reply builds relationships and audience faster than any original post.

Review the conversation map monthly as communities and accounts change.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Brand account with low engagement | Shift effort to founders and experts; brand handles support and announcements |
| Controversial topics | Decide policy in advance; avoid reactive takes outside your expertise |
| Customer complaints in public | Acknowledge quickly, move details to direct messages, follow up publicly when resolved |
| Audience moving to other networks | Test Threads, Bluesky or LinkedIn with repurposed posts |
| Reach changes | Re-test formats; avoid relying on one tactic |

## Worked example
A developer tools startup's brand account posted release notes and links to little engagement. The founder and two engineers began posting short threads on debugging lessons, replied daily to developers discussing related problems and hosted a monthly Space with users. The brand account focused on support and release highlights, quoting the engineers' best threads. Within months, follower growth from developers accelerated, sign-ups citing X rose and support complaints were resolved faster in public.

## Common mistakes
- Posting links without context.
- Never replying.
- Corporate language in a conversational feed.
- Jumping on controversies outside your expertise.
- Assuming tactics from years ago still work.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- Are your audience active on X, and in which conversations?
- Who can post and reply credibly every day?
- How will customer complaints be handled?

## Self-check before you answer
- Is the profile clear with a strong pinned post?
- Is there a conversation map and reply routine?
- Are posts specific and native?
- Are results measured and tactics re-tested?
