# International Link Building and Digital PR

A new market version starts with almost no local authority. Links from your home market help the domain overall, but local sites, local media and local directories tell search engines and people that you matter in that country. Many companies translate pages and stop there, then wonder why local competitors with weaker content outrank them. Earning local mentions takes local relationships, local stories and native outreach.

This skill plans link building and digital PR per market: local link landscapes, assets worth covering, native outreach, partnerships, local directories and associations, and measurement of local authority, all within search engine spam policies.

## Operating principles
1. **Local relevance over global authority.** A link from a respected local trade site can matter more for that market than a generic global link.
2. **Native outreach.** Pitches written and sent by native speakers who understand local media.
3. **Earn, don't buy.** Paid links and link schemes break Google's spam policies in every market.
4. **Local stories win coverage.** Local data, local customers and local angles get picked up; translated global press releases rarely do.
5. **Point links to the local version.** Mentions should link to the market's pages, not the home site.

## Local link sources
| Source | Examples | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Local media and trade press | National and industry publications in the market | High |
| Local partners and customers | Resellers, integrators, case study customers | Medium |
| Associations and chambers of commerce | Industry bodies, bilateral chambers | Medium |
| Local directories and listings | Reputable business directories, review sites | Low |
| Events and sponsorships | Local conferences, meetups, universities | Medium |
| Local experts and creators | Industry commentators, podcasts, newsletters | Medium |
| Original local data | Surveys and indexes about the market | High, high value |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it after launching a market, when local versions are indexed but rank weakly against local competitors, and when planning PR in new countries.

Don't use it to fix technical targeting (`geo-language-targeting`) or translation quality (`localisation-translation`).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Target markets and local versions.
2. Backlink profiles of your local versions and top local competitors.
3. Local assets: customers, partners, data, experts, events.
4. Native speakers or agencies available for outreach.
5. Budget and time per market.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Map the local link landscape
Which local sites link to top competitors in each market? Group by type.

### Step 2. Find linkable local assets
Local customer stories, local data, tools localised for the market, expert commentary on local issues.

### Step 3. Create one strong local asset
For example a national survey or index about the market's industry, published in the local language with local charts.

### Step 4. Build a native outreach list
Journalists, editors, associations and creators in the market, with what they cover.

### Step 5. Pitch natively
Short, relevant, in the local language, with local angles and data.

### Step 6. Activate partners and listings
Partner pages, customer case studies, association memberships and reputable local directories.

### Step 7. Measure
Local referring domains, mentions, rankings for local terms and referral traffic per market.

## Pitch structure (local language)
```
Subject: [Local data point] about [topic] in [country]
Hi [name],
A new survey of [number] [local audience] shows [finding relevant to their readers].
Key findings: [two or three local numbers]
Full data and charts (in [language]): [link to local page]
Happy to share the method or arrange a comment from [local expert].
[name], [role]
```

## Flagship local asset ideas
| Asset | Example |
|---|---|
| National survey | Late payments among Dutch small businesses |
| Market index | Annual salary or price index for the country |
| Local tool | Calculator built around local tax or regulations |
| Local research report | State of an industry in the market, with local data |
| Local expert panel | Commentary from respected local professionals |
One strong asset per market each year usually earns more local links than dozens of small pitches.

## Finding local link opportunities
1. Export backlinks of the top three local competitors in each market.
2. Filter to referring domains on the market's ccTLD or known local sites.
3. Group by type: media, trade, associations, directories, partners, events.
4. Mark sites that link to several competitors but not to you; these are the warmest targets.
5. Note what each linked to (data, tools, partnerships) to shape your asset.

## Outreach rules by market
- Learn local norms: some markets prefer formal emails, others phone or messaging apps.
- Time pitches around local holidays and news cycles.
- Offer local spokespeople or customers for comment.
- Translate the asset fully, including charts, methodology and press materials.

## Measuring local authority
| Metric | Source |
|---|---|
| New local referring domains per month | Backlink tools filtered by country or ccTLD |
| Coverage in priority publications | Media monitoring |
| Rankings for core local terms | Rank tracking by country |
| Referral traffic from local sites | Analytics |

## Partnerships that earn local links
| Partner type | Link opportunity |
|---|---|
| Resellers and integrators | Partner directory listings, co-authored guides |
| Customers | Case studies on both sites, joint webinars |
| Universities | Research collaborations, student projects, careers pages |
| Industry associations | Member directories, event sponsorship, expert articles |
| Local software or service providers | Integration pages and co-marketing |

## Directories worth listing in
Only reputable, relevant local directories and review platforms used by real customers in the market. Avoid bulk directory submission services; they add little and can look manipulative.

## Using local engine ecosystems
In Naver and Baidu markets, authority also comes from presence on the engine's own platforms: brand blogs, Q&A answers, encyclopaedia entries where eligible and community discussions. Plan these alongside traditional links, with clear disclosure of brand involvement.

Review local link progress quarterly per market.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| No local team | Use local PR freelancers or agencies with clear briefs |
| Paid publication culture in a market | Avoid paid links; label sponsored content and use rel attributes as required |
| Media links to the home site | Ask politely for the local page where relevant |
| Small markets | Focus on partners, associations and a few key publications |
| Local engines (Naver, Baidu) | Include their ecosystems: blogs, Q&A and content platforms |

## Worked example
A UK accounting software company launched in the Netherlands with translated pages that ranked poorly against Dutch competitors. The plan ran a survey of 500 Dutch small business owners on invoicing and late payments, published the results in Dutch with local charts, pitched them natively to Dutch business media and trade sites, joined a Dutch entrepreneurs' association and published case studies with Dutch accountancy partners. The survey earned coverage from several national and trade publications, local referring domains grew and the Dutch version moved onto page one for core terms within two quarters.


## Common mistakes
- Translating global press releases and sending them abroad.
- Buying links in markets where it seems common.
- Outreach in English to local media.
- Links pointing to the home site instead of local versions.
- No local stories or data.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- Who links to your top local competitors?
- What local story or data could you offer?
- Who can write and send pitches natively?

## Self-check before you answer
- Is the plan based on the local link landscape?
- Is there a local asset worth covering?
- Is outreach native and within spam policies?
- Do links point to the local version?
