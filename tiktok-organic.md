# TikTok Organic Growth

TikTok shows content to people based on what they watch and interact with, not mainly on who they follow. TikTok has explained that its For You feed is driven by user interactions, video information and device and account settings, and that follower count and past high-performing videos are not direct factors in recommendations. [Confirmed, TikTok newsroom explanation] That makes TikTok one of the few places where a new account can reach large audiences quickly, if the content holds attention. It also means consistency and creative testing matter more than polish, and many people now use TikTok as a search engine for how-tos, reviews and recommendations.

This skill builds organic TikTok programmes: account setup, positioning, content pillars, video formats, hooks, editing, sounds, captions and search keywords, posting cadence, comments and community, creator collaboration, TikTok Shop where relevant, and measurement.

## Operating principles
1. **Entertain or teach in the first seconds.** Viewers swipe instantly if the opening doesn't grab them.
2. **Native beats polished.** Phone-filmed, human, platform-style videos usually outperform ads.
3. **Volume and testing.** More videos means more chances to find what resonates.
4. **Search matters.** Keywords in captions, on-screen text and speech help discovery through TikTok search.
5. **Comments are fuel.** Reply to comments, including with video replies.

## Video formats
| Format | Example | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Tutorial or how-to | "How to fix a slow laptop in 30 seconds" | Search and saves |
| Behind the scenes | Packing orders, making products | Trust and personality |
| Storytime | Founder story, customer story | Connection |
| Myth versus fact | "3 skincare myths dermatologists hate" | Education and debate |
| Trend adaptation | A popular format applied to your niche | Reach |
| Product demo | Problem, product, result | Consideration |
| Reply to comment | Answering a viewer question on video | Community and repeat ideas |
| Duet or stitch | Responding to another video | Joining conversations |
| Series | "Day 1 of building..." | Return viewers |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it for consumer brands, creators, education, food, fashion, beauty, fitness, entertainment, local businesses and B2B brands willing to show people and personality.

Don't use it for paid campaigns (the Performance Marketing `paid-social-tiktok-linkedin` skill) or if your audience isn't there or regulations prevent it in your market.

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Goals (reach, community, traffic, sales, hiring).
2. Audience and peers or competitors on TikTok.
3. People willing to appear on camera.
4. Products, stories and expertise to show.
5. Production capacity and tools.
6. Any regulatory or brand constraints.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Set up the account
Business or creator account, clear name and bio with keywords, profile photo of a face or simple logo, link where available.

### Step 2. Define positioning and pillars
What the account is about in one sentence; three to five recurring formats or series.

### Step 3. Study the niche
Watch top videos in your niche and search results for your keywords; note hooks, formats, lengths and comments.

### Step 4. Script lightly
Hook, value, payoff; on-screen text; natural delivery.

### Step 5. Film and edit natively
Vertical, good light, clear audio, fast cuts, captions, platform text and sounds where they fit.

### Step 6. Write captions with keywords
What the video is about, in words people search.

### Step 7. Post consistently
For example three to seven times a week while testing; keep series going.

### Step 8. Engage
Reply to comments in text and video; pin helpful comments; collaborate with creators.

### Step 9. Measure and iterate
Views, average watch time, completion rate, shares, saves, comments, follows from video, profile visits and link clicks; double down on winning formats.

## Hook types
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Bold claim | "This is the fastest way to clean white trainers." |
| Question | "Why does your sourdough come out flat?" |
| Visual surprise | Showing the result first |
| Mistake | "Stop doing this when you make coffee at home." |
| Relatable situation | "POV: it's month-end and the spreadsheet won't balance." |
| Series cue | "Part 3 of fixing every room in our house for under £100." |

## Watch-time diagnosis
| What analytics show | Likely problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Most viewers leave in the first 2 seconds | Weak hook | Show the result first; stronger first line |
| Drop in the middle | Slow pacing | Cut pauses; add on-screen text and B-roll |
| Good completion, few shares | Not useful or relatable enough | Clearer takeaway; prompt to send to a friend |
| Views but few follows | No reason to come back | Series and consistent topics |

## Series ideas
- "Rating your setups" from comment submissions.
- "One tip a day" for 30 days.
- "Building X in public" with weekly updates.
- "Customer questions answered" every Friday.
Series give viewers a reason to follow and give the team a repeatable production format.

## Comment strategy
- Reply to early comments quickly after posting.
- Pin a helpful or funny comment.
- Turn good questions into video replies.
- Handle criticism calmly; correct misinformation politely.

## Repurposing across platforms
Export videos without watermarks and adapt them for Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, adjusting captions, on-screen text and length to each platform. Keep a library so strong videos can be reused months later.

## TikTok Shop
Where available and relevant, product demos linked to TikTok Shop and creator affiliate programmes can drive direct sales; follow platform rules and disclose partnerships.

## Sounds
Trending sounds can lift reach when they fit the idea, but voice-over and original audio often explain better. Check commercial use rules for business accounts before using popular music.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| B2B brand | Show people, problems and quick wins; employee-led content |
| Nobody wants to be on camera | Hands-only, voice-over, text-led or screen recordings; partner with creators |
| Regulated products | Check TikTok policies and local rules; avoid prohibited claims |
| Videos stall at low views | Test new hooks and formats; check watch time in the first seconds |
| Regulatory uncertainty about the platform | Keep content repurposable for Reels and Shorts |

## Worked example
A small homeware brand posted product videos that looked like ads, with a few hundred views each. The founder started a series filming real customer room makeovers on a budget, a weekly "rate my shelf" reply series answering comments and quick tutorials with keyword-rich captions such as "small living room ideas". Posting five times a week, several videos reached hundreds of thousands of viewers, follower growth accelerated and TikTok became a top source of new website visitors.

## Common mistakes
- Ads instead of native videos.
- Slow openings.
- Posting rarely and giving up early.
- No keywords in captions or speech.
- Ignoring comments.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- Who can appear on camera, and how often?
- What do people search for in your niche?
- What formats are working for peers?

## Self-check before you answer
- Do videos hook in the first seconds?
- Are formats native and repeatable?
- Are captions and speech keyword-aware?
- Are watch time and completion tracked and used?
