# Social Media Strategy

A social media strategy decides why a company is on social platforms, which ones, for whom, with what content and voices, and how success will be judged. Without one, teams post to fill a calendar, copy competitors, chase trends that don't fit the brand and report numbers that don't connect to the business. With one, every post serves a goal, platforms get the native content they reward and the team knows what to say no to.

This skill builds organic social strategies: goals, audiences, platform choices, positioning on social, content pillars and formats, voices (brand, employees, creators), community approach, cadence, resources, risk and measurement.

## Operating principles
1. **Pick a role for social.** Awareness, community, demand, support, hiring or reputation; each needs different content.
2. **Fewer platforms, done well.** Depth beats presence.
3. **People over logos.** Founders, employees and creators often reach and persuade more than brand pages.
4. **Native and consistent.** Content made for each platform, posted on a rhythm the team can keep.
5. **Measure what matters.** Engagement quality and business outcomes, not vanity metrics.

## Strategy components
| Component | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Goals | What should social achieve? | Grow qualified site traffic and demo requests from LinkedIn |
| Audiences | Who, and what do they care about? | Finance managers who want to close faster |
| Platforms | Where do they pay attention? | LinkedIn (primary), YouTube (secondary), X (light) |
| Social positioning | What role do we play in their feed? | The practical finance operator who shares what works |
| Pillars | Recurring themes | Month-end tips, customer stories, behind the scenes, industry takes |
| Formats | How we show up | Carousels, short video, text posts, live sessions |
| Voices | Who posts | Brand page, founder, two product experts, customers |
| Community | How we engage | Reply to every comment, weekly live Q&A, group participation |
| Cadence | How often | Three brand posts and six expert posts a week on LinkedIn |
| Measurement | How we know it works | Engagement rate, saves, profile visits, site sessions, demos |

## Platform roles (typical)
| Platform | Typical strengths |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | B2B audiences, professional expertise, employee and executive voices |
| Instagram | Visual brands, community, Reels for discovery, direct messages |
| TikTok | Discovery through short video, younger and broad audiences, search within the app |
| YouTube | Search-driven discovery, tutorials, long-form trust, Shorts |
| X | Real-time conversation, news, tech and media audiences |
| Facebook | Groups, local communities, older audiences |
| Pinterest | Planning and inspiration, evergreen visual search |
| Threads and Bluesky | Emerging conversational audiences; test lightly |
Audience behaviour varies by country and industry; confirm with your own data.

## When to use, and when not to
Use it when starting or resetting social, after an audit, when entering new markets, during annual planning and when social feels like busywork.

Don't use it for paid social plans (the Performance Marketing skills) or overall content strategy (the Content, Copy and PR `content-strategy` skill), though they should align.

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Business goals and the role social should play.
2. Audiences, personas and where they spend time.
3. Audit findings or current performance.
4. Brand positioning and voice guide.
5. People available to post (executives, experts, creators).
6. Team, budget and tools.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Define the role of social
One or two primary goals with metrics.

### Step 2. Choose platforms
Score by audience presence, fit with goals, competition and resources; pick one primary and one or two secondary.

### Step 3. Define social positioning
The role the brand plays in the feed: teacher, entertainer, insider, curator, community host.

### Step 4. Set pillars and formats
Three to five pillars, each with formats suited to each platform.

### Step 5. Choose voices
Brand page plus people: founder, experts, employees, customers and creators.

### Step 6. Plan community
Response standards, participation in conversations, groups, live sessions.

### Step 7. Set cadence and resources
Realistic posting frequency per platform and voice; production capacity.

### Step 8. Manage risk
Approval rules, crisis escalation, platform policy compliance, disclosure for partnerships.

### Step 9. Define measurement
Metrics per goal; reporting cadence.

## Platform scoring
| Platform | Audience presence (1-5) | Fit with goals (1-5) | Competition (1-5, low is good) | Our resources (1-5) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 17 |
| TikTok | 2 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 10 |

## Social positioning options
| Role | What it looks like | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher | Practical tips, tutorials, explainers | B2B, software, services |
| Insider | Behind the scenes, how things are made, team stories | Makers, food, fashion, startups |
| Entertainer | Humour, trends, characters | Consumer brands with broad audiences |
| Curator | Best links, tools and news with commentary | Media, communities, experts |
| Community host | Spotlights members, runs events and discussions | Communities, marketplaces, memberships |
Pick one primary role and keep it consistent so audiences know what to expect.

## Governance basics
- Who can post on brand accounts, and who approves what.
- Topics the brand will and won't comment on.
- Disclosure rules for partnerships and gifted products.
- Escalation path for complaints, crises and legal risks.
- Password and access management, including when people leave.

## Review cadence
Review performance monthly, pillars and formats quarterly and the full strategy yearly or when platforms or audiences shift significantly.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Very small team | One platform and one or two voices |
| B2B with conservative audience | LinkedIn and YouTube with expert voices; test short video carefully |
| Consumer brand | Short video and community first |
| Regulated industry | Pre-approved topics and templates; compliance review |
| Trends that don't fit | Skip them; consistency beats chasing |

## Worked example
A meal-kit company posted on six platforms with no clear goal. The new strategy set social's role as discovery and community for new subscribers, chose Instagram and TikTok as primary and YouTube as secondary, positioned the brand as the friendly home cook who makes weeknights easy, chose pillars of 15-minute recipes, customer kitchens, behind the scenes and seasonal ingredients, added three creator partners and set a response standard for comments. Facebook and X moved to minimal maintenance. Engagement quality and social-sourced subscriptions rose over two quarters.

## Common mistakes
- Being everywhere with the same content.
- No clear goal for social.
- Relying only on the brand page.
- Unsustainable posting plans.
- Chasing trends that don't fit the brand.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- What should social achieve for the business?
- Where does the audience pay attention?
- Who in the company can post credibly and regularly?

## Self-check before you answer
- Is there a clear role and goal for social?
- Are platforms chosen with evidence?
- Are pillars, formats and voices defined per platform?
- Is cadence realistic and measurement tied to goals?
