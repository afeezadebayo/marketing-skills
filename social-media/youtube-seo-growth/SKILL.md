# YouTube SEO and Channel Growth

YouTube is both a social platform and one of the world's largest search engines. People come to learn, compare products, fix problems and be entertained, and videos keep earning views for years when they answer real searches. YouTube has explained that its recommendations aim to match viewers with videos they will value, using signals such as what people watch, how long they watch, what they skip and survey responses about satisfaction. [Confirmed, YouTube] Titles, thumbnails and the first 30 seconds decide whether people click and stay; metadata helps YouTube understand what a video is about.

This skill grows YouTube channels: channel setup, topic and keyword research, video planning, titles, thumbnails, hooks and retention, descriptions, chapters and tags, Shorts, playlists, community, YouTube's appearance in Google results and measurement.

## Operating principles
1. **Answer real searches and interests.** Topics come from what viewers look for and care about.
2. **Packaging decides clicks.** Title and thumbnail work together to promise value.
3. **Retention decides reach.** Deliver on the promise quickly and keep pacing tight.
4. **Series and playlists build habits.** Viewers who watch several videos become subscribers.
5. **Measure satisfaction, not just views.** Watch time, returning viewers and comments show value.

## Channel elements
| Element | Good practice |
|---|---|
| Channel name and handle | Clear, memorable, consistent with brand |
| Banner | What the channel offers and how often |
| Description | Who it's for, topics, keywords, links |
| Channel trailer or featured video | Best introduction for new visitors |
| Playlists | Organised by topic or series |
| Sections | Popular, new, series and Shorts on the home tab |

## Video SEO elements
| Element | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Title | Clear promise with the main keyword, usually under about 60 characters to avoid truncation |
| Thumbnail | Large readable text (few words), a face or result, strong contrast; complements the title |
| Hook (first 30 seconds) | Confirm the promise, preview the value, skip long intros |
| Description | First lines summarise the video with keywords; links; timestamps |
| Chapters | Timestamps for key sections; help viewers and search |
| Captions | Accurate subtitles for accessibility and understanding |
| Tags | Minor role; include main terms and common misspellings |
| End screens and cards | Point to the next relevant video or playlist |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it for tutorials, product education, reviews, thought leadership, recruiting, customer support content and Shorts.

Don't use it for YouTube ads (the Performance Marketing skills) or if the team can't commit to regular video production.

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Goals (audience, leads, support deflection, brand).
2. Channel analytics if it exists.
3. Audience and their questions.
4. Competitor or peer channels.
5. People who can present and production capacity.
6. Existing videos, webinars or talks to reuse.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Set up or fix the channel
Name, handle, banner, description, trailer, playlists and home tab sections.

### Step 2. Research topics
YouTube search suggestions, competitor top videos, comments, Google results with video carousels and customer questions.

### Step 3. Plan videos and series
Pillar series (tutorials, reviews, explainers, case studies) and a Shorts plan.

### Step 4. Package before filming
Write titles and sketch thumbnails first; if the packaging isn't compelling, rethink the idea.

### Step 5. Script for retention
Hook in 30 seconds, clear structure, visual changes, pattern interrupts, no padding.

### Step 6. Publish with metadata
Description, chapters, captions, tags, end screens, cards and playlists.

### Step 7. Promote
Newsletter, social clips, website embeds, community posts.

### Step 8. Engage
Reply to comments, pin helpful ones, ask questions, use community posts.

### Step 9. Measure and iterate
Click-through rate, average view duration, retention graphs, returning viewers, subscribers per video, traffic sources.

## Retention fixes
| Retention graph pattern | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Big drop in the first 30 seconds | Hook doesn't match title or thumbnail | Confirm the promise immediately |
| Steady decline | Slow pacing | Tighter edits, more visuals |
| Sudden drop at a point | Off-topic section or long sponsor read | Cut or move it |
| Spikes | Viewers rewatching | Possibly confusing or highly valuable; review |

## Title and thumbnail pairs
| Title | Thumbnail idea |
|---|---|
| How to Reconcile Bank Feeds in 10 Minutes | Split screen: messy spreadsheet vs clean dashboard, text "10 MIN" |
| 5 Month-End Mistakes Finance Teams Make | Presenter pointing at a red X, text "STOP" |
| Xero vs QuickBooks for Small Agencies | Both logos with a scale, text "Which?" |
The thumbnail should add to the title rather than repeat it.

## Description template
```
First 2 lines: what viewers will learn, with the main keyword.
Resources and links mentioned.
Chapters:
0:00 Intro
0:30 Step 1
...
About the channel and subscribe prompt.
```

## Shorts strategy
Use Shorts for quick tips, highlights from long videos and trend-led ideas. Link each Short to a related long video, keep Shorts on topics that match the channel and watch whether Shorts viewers go on to watch long-form content.

## Metrics
| Metric | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Impressions click-through rate | Packaging strength |
| Average view duration and percentage | Content holds attention |
| Retention graph | Where viewers leave |
| Returning viewers | Channel habit |
| Subscribers gained per video | Audience building |
| Traffic sources (search, suggested, browse, external) | How people find videos |
| Clicks to website and conversions | Business value |

## Videos in Google search
Clear titles, descriptions, chapters and transcripts help videos appear in Google video results and key moments. Embedding videos on relevant pages of your site with video structured data can bring additional visibility.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| B2B niche | Tutorials and problem-solving videos for small, valuable audiences |
| Low production capacity | Repurpose webinars into chapters and Shorts |
| Faceless channel | Screen recordings and voice-over can work for tutorials |
| Low click-through | Test new thumbnails and titles (YouTube offers thumbnail testing for many channels) |
| Shorts without long-form growth | Link Shorts to related long videos |

## Worked example
A B2B accounting software company uploaded webinar recordings with titles like "Q3 Product Webinar" and few views. Topic research found strong search demand for specific how-tos such as reconciling bank feeds and month-end checklists. The new channel plan launched a weekly tutorial series with keyword-led titles, clear thumbnails, 30-second hooks and chapters, cut webinars into focused videos and Shorts, and organised playlists by task. Within a year, views and watch time grew several times over, the videos appeared in Google video results for target queries, and support tickets for the covered tasks fell.

## Common mistakes
- Vague titles like "Webinar recording".
- Thumbnails that are unreadable on phones.
- Long intros before the value.
- No playlists or end screens.
- Judging videos only by views.

## Questions to ask before you plan
- What do your viewers search for and struggle with?
- Who can present on camera regularly?
- Which existing videos or webinars can be reused?

## Self-check before you answer
- Are topics based on search and audience interest?
- Are titles and thumbnails planned before filming?
- Do scripts hook within 30 seconds?
- Are retention and satisfaction metrics used?
