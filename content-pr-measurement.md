# Content and PR Measurement

Content and PR are notoriously hard to measure, so teams often report what is easy: pageviews, impressions, followers, number of press releases and "advertising value equivalent" for coverage. None of these tell leadership whether content and PR helped the business. Good measurement connects activity to outcomes (awareness among the right audience, demand, pipeline, sales enablement, retention, reputation) while being honest that much of content's effect is indirect and long-term.

This skill builds measurement for content and PR: goals and metrics by objective, tracking setup, attribution approaches, coverage quality scoring, content scorecards, reporting cadence and decisions. The Barcelona Principles from AMEC, the international communications measurement body, reject advertising value equivalents and call for outcome-focused measurement. [Industry standard, AMEC]

## Operating principles
1. **Measure against goals.** Each objective has its own metrics.
2. **Outcomes over outputs.** Leads, pipeline and reputation over volume of content or coverage.
3. **Quality over quantity.** Coverage and links in relevant, authoritative places matter most.
4. **Combine data and judgement.** Attribution is imperfect; use several signals and qualitative evidence.
5. **Report decisions, not just numbers.** Each report should change what the team does next.

## Metrics by objective
| Objective | Leading metrics | Outcome metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach among target audience, branded search, share of voice | Aided and unaided awareness (surveys) |
| Engagement | Engaged time, scroll depth, returning visitors, newsletter clicks | Subscribers retained, community growth |
| Demand | Content-sourced sign-ups, demo requests | Pipeline and revenue influenced |
| Sales enablement | Content used in deals, shares by sales | Win rate and cycle length where content used |
| Retention | Help content usage, customer newsletter engagement | Churn and expansion among engaged customers |
| Reputation and PR | Coverage in target outlets, message pull-through, spokesperson quotes | Trust and perception survey results |
| Search and AI visibility | Links, mentions, citations in AI answers | Organic traffic and branded queries |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it to set up content and PR reporting, justify budgets, evaluate agencies, decide what to produce next and align with sales and finance.

Don't use it for paid media reporting (the Performance Marketing `paid-media-reporting` skill) or SEO performance analysis alone (the Content SEO skills).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. Content and PR goals.
2. Analytics, CRM and marketing automation access.
3. Media monitoring and backlink tools.
4. Content inventory with types and topics.
5. Survey data on awareness or perception, if any.
6. Stakeholders and decisions they make.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Agree goals and metrics
One or two outcome metrics per objective; leading indicators to watch monthly.

### Step 2. Set up tracking
UTM conventions, content groups in analytics, CRM fields for content touches, newsletter tracking, coverage logging.

### Step 3. Choose attribution approaches
First-touch and multi-touch views from analytics and CRM, self-reported attribution ("How did you hear about us?"), assisted conversions, and, for larger programmes, lift studies or marketing mix models.

### Step 4. Score coverage
Relevance, authority, message pull-through, links and spokesperson quotes; no advertising value equivalents.

### Step 5. Build a content scorecard
Performance by piece, topic, format and pillar against goals.

### Step 6. Report
Monthly operational report; quarterly business review with decisions.

### Step 7. Decide
What to produce more of, update, repurpose or stop.

## Self-reported attribution
Add an open field to sign-up and demo forms: "How did you hear about us?" Answers often reveal podcasts, newsletters, communities, word of mouth and coverage that analytics misses. Code answers monthly and compare with analytics attribution.

## Content scorecard
```
| Piece | Pillar | Format | Sessions | Engaged time | Sign-ups | Assisted pipeline | Links | Sales usage | Decision |
```

## Tracking setup checklist
- UTM naming convention for every channel and campaign, documented and shared.
- Content groups in analytics by pillar, format and funnel stage.
- Conversion events for sign-ups, demos, downloads and newsletter subscriptions.
- CRM fields or campaign membership recording content touches before deals.
- Coverage log with outlet, date, link, quality score and message pull-through.
- Backlink monitoring for content and PR assets.
- Self-reported attribution field on key forms.

## Coverage quality score
| Criterion | Score 0-2 |
|---|---|
| Audience relevance | Outlet readers match target customers |
| Authority | Outlet respected in the field |
| Message pull-through | Key messages or data included |
| Spokesperson | Expert quoted by name |
| Link | Followed link to a relevant page |
Report average scores and the number of high-quality pieces rather than raw counts.

## Influence analysis
Compare conversion rates and deal sizes for buyers who engaged with content (for example two or more guides or newsletter clicks) against similar buyers who did not. This is correlation, not proof, but it shows which content tends to accompany successful deals and is useful alongside self-reported answers.

## Reporting cadence
| Report | Audience | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Content and PR team | Leading metrics, top and bottom pieces, coverage log |
| Quarterly | Leadership | Outcomes, influence, coverage quality, decisions |
| Yearly | Leadership and finance | Strategy review, budget allocation, research plans |

## Awareness and perception surveys
For larger programmes, run a short survey of the target audience once or twice a year measuring aided and unaided awareness and perception of the brand on key attributes. Changes over time show whether content and PR are shifting reputation, which web analytics alone can't show.

## Decisions from measurement
Each quarterly review should end with a short list: formats and topics to produce more of, pieces to update or repurpose, activities to stop, budget shifts and any tracking gaps to fix before the next quarter.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Long B2B cycles | Track content touches in CRM and report influence over quarters |
| Leadership wants ROI | Show influenced pipeline with ranges plus qualitative evidence |
| Agency reports impressions | Ask for coverage quality, links and outcome metrics |
| Dark social (private sharing) | Use self-reported attribution and branded search trends |
| Small team | Track a few metrics well rather than many badly |

## Worked example
A B2B company's content team reported pageviews and press release counts, and leadership questioned the budget. The new measurement agreed goals for demand and reputation, added CRM tracking of content touches, a "How did you hear about us?" field and coverage quality scoring. Quarterly reports showed that buyers who read two or more guides converted at much higher rates, the annual research report drove most top-tier coverage and links, and the podcast appeared often in self-reported answers despite low tracked traffic. Budget shifted towards research, guides and the podcast, and away from routine press releases.


## Common mistakes
- Reporting pageviews and impressions as success.
- Using advertising value equivalents for PR.
- Relying on last-click attribution alone.
- Ignoring self-reported and qualitative evidence.
- Reports that don't lead to decisions.

## Questions to ask before you measure
- What should content and PR achieve for the business?
- Which data sources can connect content to outcomes?
- What decisions will reports inform?

## Self-check before you answer
- Are metrics tied to objectives?
- Are outcomes, not just outputs, measured?
- Is coverage scored for quality?
- Do reports end with decisions?
